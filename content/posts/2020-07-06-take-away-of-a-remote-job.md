Due to Covid-19, many industries started practicing remote work all over the world. Honestly, it was a much-awaited test in the software industry, even though a few companies were successful in a completely remote job already.

We believe that folks who have decided to shift their career trajectory, know what they want, and what they need to do to achieve it. Having a solid commitment to learning, the self-knowledge to be able to learn efficiently, and so on.

I have recently joined an Indonesian based Insurance company. With my colleagues, I am Working on improving the web experience of our platform.
The day I landed Indonesia, from the next day lockdown has been implemented, and we started working from home. I was very happy to start this journey as it's a new country, team, and product (new business for me). Benefits of remote jobs are you don't need to commute for your work, you can attend your standup when you are trying to cook (hey come on, dishes which I have prepared are eatable only), mid-day shower, having nap whenever we need.

Besides all I was having a bit of fear, there is no direct physical contact if I get stuck anywhere in the work or anything.

With all those fears I have made up my mind to build new habits that are necessary for a remote job to avoid feeling burst out and avoid getting imposter syndrome. For the remote job, we need to be motivated.

In this blog, I have tried to summarize the things which have helped me as an immature remote developer.

# 1. Ask more questions:

When you take up any new task, to make yourself understand the requirements you need to ask a lot of questions to clarify the assumptions.

Whenever you want to clarify your questions, don't just simply shoot your abstract questions. Instead started conveying them that what you have understood, your thought process on the problem which you are trying to solve, and then ask your colleagues to validate it.

This approach will help you not miss anything, and it will help your colleagues to fill the gaps easily.

Eventually, it will result positively, because explaining your collective thoughts is a good expression of gaining clarity.

# 2. Focus and Patience:

Whenever you move to a different product, you will have a deep learning curve. It might be a new tech stack, new language (or) new design pattern (or) new architecture (or) new framework.

You need to make up your mind like, let's learn everything slowly and completely.

You can't learn everything at a time you need to stop yourself rushing too much. An ideal way can help is, based on the demand of the current sprint learn the things (it could be product knowledge or new tech stack).

This may take multiple weeks or even months, but a good indicator that you’ve reached a comfortable level is that you’re able to provide valuable feedback to pull requests. The other indicator is that you now know how to solve the problem quickly and with minor comments on your pull requests.

# 3. Value your colleague's time and your's too:

This part is so crucial, you need to be well prepared for every day. If you have your colleagues at different time zone, I would say if you fail to approach properly with this area. You will end up in burst out.

This is a highly valued skill when it comes to remote work. Your performance will be measured by results rather than time spent (or maybe both). 

Sharing your struggles to colleagues which can help you solve your problem on-time, these are necessary for a remote team to work cohesively because others will depend on your work.

# Closing thoughts:

The remote working environment is completely different from our previous days. This evolving atmosphere means a new way of looking at getting work done.

As a remote worker, you have to be able to work independently and proactively. Based on the time difference, there might be times when you won’t have anyone around to ask a particular question. A little proactivity will take you a long way.
