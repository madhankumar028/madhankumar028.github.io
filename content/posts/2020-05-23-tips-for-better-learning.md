Learning is incredibly personal, so what works for me may not work exactly for you. This isn’t a recipe, consider this is a playbook. I’ve put together a few tips from my own experience that I believe it will benefit your practice of daily learning.

We have been grown from our school days by learning for exams or whatever (to achieve specific thing), I guess from there it's all started. But when you come out of those things you will have different perceptions over learning. The reason why I am telling it as a different perception is, most of us would have pulled the major things in a night before the day, or over a cramming session. But the actual learning is not like that.

I am grouping the learning into two factors,

1. Consumption
2. Retention

### Consumption:

As part of consumption, I will take a dose every day,

```
Appreciate that you do not know everything — and you actually do not need to.
```

Adopting a beginner’s mindset will keep you more enthusiast, increase creativity, and gratitude. 

To keep the continual learning process, I will go through my twitter feeds, blogs, telegram channels, and subscribed to a few newsletters. I stay active on interesting discussion forums. When it comes to consumption we have to take care of who we follow, who we engage with, whose ideas we are taking into.

### Retention:

As Feynman said, 
```
“If you can’t explain it in simple terms, then you don’t understand it.”
```

If you have to retain the knowledge what you have learnt, the better way is to teach others. Teaching is not always necessarily a kind of lecture. Share it with your colleagues, or post it in a forum and create a conversation based on it. That's it you have started teaching. Eventually sometimes you will end up teaching it to someone or learning it from others.

Teaching is going to be an ultimate test case for your knowledge to convey it to another.

![](https://miro.medium.com/max/516/0*FS77IsK_PCpQwMIP.jpeg)

**Learning pyramid here,**

1. 90% of what they learn when they teach someone else/use immediately.

2. 75% of what they learn when they practice what they learned.

3. 50% of what they learn when engaged in a group discussion.

4. 30% of what they learn when they see a demonstration.


When we start distill a concept into its basic parts not only boosts understanding, it also fills the knowledge gaps.
If you feel any gaps, go to consumption and then come back to retention.


## Closing thoughts:

For Consumption and Retention you don't need dedicated time, just make it as your habit.

Keep learning. What you have learnt yesterday may be obsolete today or tomorrow. So keep learning as your daily routine.
